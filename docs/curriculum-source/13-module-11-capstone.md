# Module 11 Capstone: Preliminary High-Current DC Engineering Assessment

**Objective:** Demonstrate the ability to evaluate the electrical architecture, principal hazards, and certification uncertainties of Product B before committing to detailed engineering.

Prepare a preliminary engineering assessment for the proposed battery-assisted sauna heater.

The report must include:

1. **Electrical requirements:** Battery capacity, nominal voltage, operating voltage range, DC heating power, AC heating power, charging requirements, and expected operating modes.

2. **Power and current calculations:** Calculate nominal current for 6, 8, and 10 kW DC loads at 48V, 96V, and 192V. Explain how voltage variation, fixed-resistance heating elements, and power regulation affect actual current.

3. **Battery performance:** Compare 5, 7.5, and 10 kWh LFP battery configurations. Estimate C-rate, ideal discharge duration, usable energy, and relevant battery performance limitations.

4. **Electrical distribution:** Compare conceptual busbar and cable-based architectures. Evaluate resistance, voltage drop, thermal losses, connection reliability, mechanical constraints, and serviceability.

5. **Electrical protection:** Identify the major protection functions required for the battery, distribution system, DC heating circuits, and charger. Distinguish overload protection, short-circuit protection, protective disconnection, and normal switching.

6. **Switching and controls:** Describe the functions of relays, contactors, electronic switches, and protective controls. Analyze the consequences of a switching device failing open or closed.

7. **Fault analysis:** Identify credible electrical hazards, including short circuits, loose connections, contactor welding, insulation failures, loss of control, and BMS faults. Explain which hazards could produce overheating, arcing, or fire.

8. **Charging and AC power management:** Evaluate how the AC heating subsystem and battery charger share a residential 120V input. Identify operating modes that could exceed available circuit capacity and the protective functions that would require evaluation.

9. **Grounding, bonding, and isolation:** Identify the major questions concerning protective grounding, electrical isolation, service disconnects, and safe maintenance.

10. **Thermal integration:** Discuss how high-current electrical components and the battery generate heat, and how proximity to the sauna heater affects component suitability and system safety.

11. **Architecture comparison:** Compare at least three candidate configurations:
    - Nominal 48V integrated battery and heater.
    - Higher-voltage battery architecture.
    - Separate battery enclosure connected to the heating appliance.

12. **Certification considerations:** Identify candidate standards for the battery, electrical distribution, controls, charger, and finished sauna appliance. Distinguish confirmed applicability from unresolved questions.

13. **Verification requirements:** Prepare a preliminary verification matrix linking major engineering requirements to the evidence needed to evaluate them. Do not invent formal certification acceptance criteria.

14. **Uncertainty register:** Record engineering assumptions, missing component data, unresolved standards questions, and decisions requiring review by qualified engineers or certification laboratories.

15. **Recommended next steps:** Prioritize the technical and regulatory questions that should be resolved before selecting the final architecture or committing substantial manufacturing expenditure.

## Final mastery questions

- [ ] Can I calculate current, power, resistance, and losses in a high-current DC system?
- [ ] Can I explain why a nominal 48V, 10 kW system presents substantial electrical and thermal challenges?
- [ ] Can I explain the differences between nominal and actual battery voltage?
- [ ] Can I explain how fixed-resistance and constant-power loads behave differently as voltage changes?
- [ ] Can I calculate battery C-rate and approximate discharge duration?
- [ ] Can I identify the principal factors affecting conductor and connection suitability?
- [ ] Can I explain why connection resistance can create dangerous localized heating?
- [ ] Can I distinguish normal switching from protective fault interruption?
- [ ] Can I explain why DC-rated protective devices are necessary?
- [ ] Can I identify the consequences of a welded contactor?
- [ ] Can I explain why a BMS is not automatically sufficient to protect the entire heating appliance?
- [ ] Can I identify the main considerations for grounding, bonding, and electrical isolation?
- [ ] Can I explain how battery charging and AC heating compete for limited residential input power?
- [ ] Can I compare 48V and higher-voltage architectures without assuming either is universally superior?
- [ ] Can I identify the principal hazards of integrating a battery with a high-temperature appliance?
- [ ] Can I distinguish confirmed technical requirements from assumptions and unresolved certification questions?
- [ ] Can I explain the proposed architecture to an experienced electrical engineer or NRTL project engineer?
- [ ] Can I identify which engineering decisions should be deferred until the certification pathway is better understood?

## Capstone completion criteria

Mark Module 11 complete only when I can:

1. Explain the electrical architecture of Product B without relying on memorized component descriptions.
2. Perform the relevant power, current, energy, and loss calculations.
3. Identify major safety hazards and explain the engineering principles behind them.
4. Compare reasonable architecture alternatives and articulate their tradeoffs.
5. Identify the information required to evaluate protective devices and components.
6. Explain how the AC input, charger, battery, and heating subsystems interact.
7. Prepare a technically credible preliminary standards-applicability inquiry.
8. Distinguish conclusions supported by evidence from questions requiring qualified engineering or laboratory review.

The capstone is an educational and preliminary engineering assessment. It does not constitute a certified design, installation authorization, or permission to perform hazardous high-current testing.
