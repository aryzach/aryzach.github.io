Curriculum Content: Modules 17–20
Continuation of the Codex curriculum specification, with specific topics, readings, summaries, and mastery questions.
Module 17: Legal Installation, Product Liability, and Commercial Deployment
Objective: Understand when an electrical appliance can legally be installed, rented, or sold in US homes, and how manufacturers and installers manage regulatory and liability exposure.
Topic 17.1: Installation legality
Readings
1. NFPA 70: National Electrical Code
Summary: Study NEC Articles 100, 110, 210, and 422. Focus on equipment approval, listing, installation instructions, appliance requirements, and the distinction between cord-connected and permanently connected equipment.
2. California Building Standards Commission
Summary: Understand how California adopts and modifies model building, electrical, and fire codes. Identify the code editions applicable to residential appliance installations.
3. San Francisco Department of Building Inspection
Summary: Learn how local permitting, inspections, interpretations, and alternative approval processes operate.
Questions
- [ ] What conditions make an electrical appliance legally installable in a US residence?
- [ ] Does using an existing receptacle eliminate product approval requirements?
- [ ] What's the difference between a permit exemption and exemption from electrical safety requirements?
- [ ] Can an AHJ approve an unlisted appliance?
- [ ] What are the legal differences between a cord-connected heater and a hardwired heater?
- [ ] Does a certified heater automatically make the complete sauna cabin compliant?
- [ ] Which code sections and local determinations are most relevant to SF Sauna?
Topic 17.2: Field evaluations and alternative approval
Readings
1. UL Solutions: Field Evaluations
Summary: Explains evaluations of equipment outside conventional product certification, including how evaluation results can support approval by an AHJ.
2. OSHA: NRTL Program
Summary: Explains the recognition of testing laboratories and the regulatory role of their certifications.
3. San Francisco DBI
Summary: Consult local requirements for alternative materials, methods, equipment approval, and inspection.
Questions
- [ ] How does field evaluation differ from a conventional NRTL product listing?
- [ ] Does a field evaluation approve one installation or an entire product model?
- [ ] What evidence might an AHJ require?
- [ ] Can field evaluation be a practical pathway for a rental business with dozens of installations?
- [ ] What are the limitations of alternative approval?
- [ ] What would need to be confirmed in writing before relying on this approach?
Topic 17.3: Manufacturer, importer, and installer responsibilities
Readings
1. CPSC: Business and Manufacturing
Summary: Introduces federal consumer product safety responsibilities, including reporting, recalls, and manufacturer obligations.
2. CBP: Basic Importing and Exporting
Summary: Explains the customs and import framework, including importer responsibilities.
3. OSHA: NRTL Program
Summary: Useful for distinguishing workplace electrical equipment approval from residential consumer product regulation.
Questions
- [ ] What responsibilities does a US importer assume when purchasing heaters from a Chinese factory?
- [ ] Does purchasing a product DDP eliminate the importer's potential product liability?
- [ ] How do manufacturer, distributor, installer, and lessor responsibilities differ?
- [ ] Does selling an appliance create different obligations from renting it?
- [ ] Who is responsible for verifying installation conditions?
- [ ] What documentation should be retained for each installed sauna?
Topic 17.4: Product liability and insurance
Readings
1. Cornell Legal Information Institute: Products Liability
Summary: Overview of liability for defective products, including manufacturing defects, design defects, and inadequate warnings.
2. CPSC: Reporting Unsafe Products
Summary: Explains manufacturers' and other regulated parties' duties to report certain product hazards and defects.
3. CPSC: Recall Guidance
Summary: Explains product recall processes and corrective actions.
Questions
- [ ] What is the difference between a design defect and a manufacturing defect?
- [ ] Can a certified appliance still create product liability?
- [ ] Can an uncertified appliance be legally sold but still expose the seller to substantial liability?
- [ ] How might a failure-to-warn claim arise?
- [ ] What happens if a sauna causes a residential fire?
- [ ] Under what circumstances might an owner or founder face personal liability?
- [ ] How do general liability and product liability insurance differ?
- [ ] What evidence should be preserved after an incident?
Topic 17.5: Recalls and post-market safety
Readings
1. CPSC Recalls Database
Summary: Search documented recalls of electric heaters, heating appliances, sauna products, batteries, and cooking equipment.
2. CPSC: Recall Guidance
Summary: Study reporting obligations, corrective-action plans, consumer notification, and product recovery.
Questions
- [ ] What types of defects trigger reporting obligations?
- [ ] What distinguishes a field repair campaign from a formal recall?
- [ ] What information should a manufacturer collect about field failures?
- [ ] How should repeated timer or terminal failures be investigated?
- [ ] When might continued installation or distribution become inappropriate?
- [ ] How does a manufacturer demonstrate that a corrective action worked?
Module capstone: Prepare a legal and regulatory due-diligence memo for SF Sauna's 120V heater installations, distinguishing verified requirements from questions requiring written confirmation by regulators, insurers, or counsel.

Module 18: Certification Project Management
Objective: Understand how to take an electrical appliance from an early prototype through NRTL certification and maintain its certification during manufacturing.
Topic 18.1: Choosing a certification laboratory
Readings
1. OSHA: Recognized NRTLs
Summary: Identify recognized laboratories and understand that recognition must be verified for the relevant standards and activities.
2. UL Solutions: Product Certification
Summary: Overview of certification programs and product evaluation.
3. Intertek: Electrical Product Certification
Summary: Explains ETL certification and the role of third-party evaluation.
4. SGS: Product Certification
Summary: Investigate SGS's electrical product certification capabilities and applicable recognition scope.
Questions
- [ ] What is the practical difference between UL, ETL, SGS, and other NRTL certification marks?
- [ ] How do you verify whether a laboratory can certify a particular product to a particular standard?
- [ ] What is the difference between a testing laboratory and a certification body?
- [ ] Can multiple laboratories participate in one product's evaluation?
- [ ] What factors should influence laboratory selection besides cost?
- [ ] Why might a laboratory decline a novel product?
Topic 18.2: Determining applicable standards
Readings
1. UL Standards Catalog
Summary: Investigate the scopes of UL 875, UL 60335-2-53, UL 60335-1, UL 499, and relevant battery standards.
2. IEC Webstore
Summary: Compare IEC standards and editions with their US counterparts.
3. IECEE CB Scheme
Summary: Learn how international test reports and certificates may support national certification.
Questions
- [ ] Who determines which standard applies to a new product?
- [ ] Can a manufacturer choose a less demanding standard simply because it is cheaper?
- [ ] How do product scope and exclusions affect certification?
- [ ] What happens when a product combines two traditionally separate product categories?
- [ ] How are gaps in existing standards handled?
- [ ] What happens when a standard is revised during a certification project?
- [ ] What can be learned from HUUM's SGS certification approach?
Topic 18.3: Preparing a certification application
Readings
1. UL Solutions: Certification Services
Summary: Review the certification process and typical documentation requirements.
2. Intertek: ETL Listed Mark
Summary: Understand how product certification relates to approved construction and ongoing compliance.
Questions
- [ ] What information should be included in a product description?
- [ ] Why are schematics and bills of materials necessary?
- [ ] What is a safety-critical component list?
- [ ] What documentation is needed for temperature controls?
- [ ] How should multiple wattage variants be presented?
- [ ] What constitutes a product family?
- [ ] How should the certification scope distinguish a heater from a complete sauna cabin?
Topic 18.4: Testing, failure correction, and retesting
Readings
1. UL Solutions: Certification
Summary: Review evaluation and certification stages.
2. UL 875 and UL 60335-2-53
Summary: Study the relevant testing requirements using authorized access, distinguishing construction requirements, normal operation, abnormal operation, and acceptance criteria.
Questions
- [ ] What happens when a prototype fails a thermal test?
- [ ] Who determines whether a design change requires retesting?
- [ ] Can a manufacturer substitute components during evaluation?
- [ ] How should test failures be documented?
- [ ] How can precompliance testing reduce certification uncertainty?
- [ ] Why might a seemingly minor design change affect multiple tests?
Topic 18.5: Factory inspections and maintaining certification
Readings
1. UL Solutions: Follow-Up Services
Summary: Study how ongoing factory oversight helps ensure that production products remain consistent with certified construction.
2. ISO 9001
Summary: General quality-management principles applicable to manufacturing documentation, traceability, and corrective action.
Questions
- [ ] Why does certification involve more than testing a prototype?
- [ ] What happens if the manufacturer changes a relay, timer, or heating element?
- [ ] Can the factory change its wire supplier without notifying the certification body?
- [ ] How are alternate components approved?
- [ ] What production records are useful during an inspection?
- [ ] What happens if a certified product no longer matches its approved construction?
Module capstone: Prepare a preliminary certification application package for Product A, including a product description, standards-applicability matrix, safety-critical BOM template, test plan, and list of questions for three NRTLs.

Module 19: Real-World Failures, Recalls, and Regulatory Case Studies
Objective: Learn from actual product failures and certification experiences, focusing on how engineering decisions, manufacturing processes, and regulatory systems interact.
Use documented incidents. Distinguish established causes from speculation.
Topic 19.1: Electric heater fires and thermal failures
Readings
1. CPSC Recalls: Electric Heaters
Summary: Search for electric heater recalls involving overheating, fire hazards, faulty controls, and electrical connections.
2. NFPA Research
Summary: Review fire research and statistics concerning heating equipment and residential electrical fires.
Questions
- [ ] What failure mechanisms commonly cause electric heaters to become fire hazards?
- [ ] How can a product overheat without tripping a branch-circuit breaker?
- [ ] Why might an appliance pass normal-operation tests but fail abnormal-operation tests?
- [ ] What kinds of failures can independent protective controls mitigate?
- [ ] What evidence is needed to establish the cause of a fire?
Topic 19.2: Lithium-ion battery fires
Readings
1. UL Solutions: UL 9540A
Summary: Introduces thermal runaway propagation testing for energy storage technologies.
2. NFPA 855
Summary: Study the installation safety framework for stationary energy storage systems.
3. CPSC Recalls
Summary: Identify documented battery-related recalls involving fire or thermal hazards.
Questions
- [ ] What initiates thermal runaway?
- [ ] Why can a battery fire continue after electrical power is disconnected?
- [ ] What distinguishes cell failure from pack-level propagation?
- [ ] How does battery chemistry affect risk?
- [ ] What does UL 9540A actually evaluate?
- [ ] What design lessons are relevant to a battery-assisted sauna heater?
