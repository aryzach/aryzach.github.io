# Topic 2.6: Residential Circuit Loading, Plug Configurations, and Installation Constraints

**Objective:** Understand the practical and regulatory limits of powering residential sauna heaters from existing 120V branch circuits.

### Readings

**1. [NFPA 70: National Electrical Code](https://www.nfpa.org/codes-and-standards/nfpa-70-standard-development/70)**

**Summary:** Study Articles 100, 110, 210, 240, 250, 400, and 422. Focus on continuous-load definitions, branch-circuit sizing, appliance ratings, receptacle requirements, grounding, flexible cords, and installation instructions. The precise requirements depend on the adopted edition and equipment classification.

**2. [NEMA Standards](https://www.nema.org/standards)**

**Summary:** Investigate NEMA 5-15 and 5-20 plug and receptacle configurations. Understand their nominal ratings, physical differences, and intended applications.

**3. [California Building Standards Commission](https://www.dgs.ca.gov/BSC/Codes)**

**Summary:** Learn how California adopts and amends the electrical code, and why national NEC guidance must be checked against the locally effective edition.

**4. [San Francisco Department of Building Inspection](https://www.sf.gov/departments/department-building-inspection)**

**Summary:** Investigate local requirements for residential appliance installations, permits, equipment approval, and inspections.

### Mastery questions

- [ ] What is the NEC definition of a continuous load?
- [ ] Why does the three-hour definition not create a general permission to overload a circuit for shorter periods?
- [ ] How does the conventional 125% continuous-load sizing rule work?
- [ ] What exceptions or equipment-specific rules might affect the analysis?
- [ ] What is the difference between a 15A branch circuit and a 20A branch circuit?
- [ ] What is the difference between NEMA 5-15 and NEMA 5-20 connectors?
- [ ] How much current does a 1.7 kW heater draw at 120V?
- [ ] How much current does a 2.0 kW heater draw?
- [ ] How much current does a 2.2 kW heater draw?
- [ ] Why is a 20A breaker not sufficient evidence that a particular appliance is suitable for that circuit?
- [ ] What is the significance of a dedicated circuit?
- [ ] When might GFCI protection be required?
- [ ] What installation information must be provided by the appliance manufacturer?
- [ ] Why does using an existing receptacle not automatically resolve product approval requirements?
- [ ] What additional information would be required to establish whether Product A can be installed legally in San Francisco?

### Applied exercise

Prepare an electrical feasibility matrix for heaters rated at 1.2, 1.5, 1.7, 1.8, 2.0, and 2.2 kW.

For each variant, record:

- Nominal current at 120V.
- Potential circuit configuration.
- Relevant continuous-load considerations.
- Plug and receptacle questions.
- Certification questions.
- Installation restrictions.
- Unresolved code interpretations.

Do not declare a configuration compliant without the necessary evidence.
